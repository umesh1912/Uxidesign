Enterprise SaaS · Healthcare

Claim Management Software

End-to-end UX redesign of a complex insurance claim processing platform for healthcare providers — reducing claim rejection rates and accelerating reimbursement cycles.

My RoleLead Product Designer + Scrum Master
Duration6 Months
PlatformWeb App (Desktop-first)
ToolsFigma · FigJam · Claude.ai · Storybook
01 · Research

Understanding the Claims Chaos

Conducted 12 stakeholder interviews with billing specialists, practice managers, and insurance coordinators. Used Claude.ai to synthesise 40+ hours of interview transcripts into structured insight clusters. Ran VOC analysis to identify the top pain points: a 34% claim rejection rate due to coding errors, a fragmented multi-screen workflow requiring 11 clicks per claim submission, and zero real-time validation feedback.

34%Avg. claim rejection rate
11Clicks per submission
3Disconnected legacy screens
0Real-time validation
02 · Define

Framing the Problem

Created 3 user personas — the Billing Specialist, the Practice Manager, and the Compliance Officer — each with distinct goals and frustrations. Mapped a current-state journey exposing 6 critical failure points where claims fell through. Defined the design challenge: "How might we give billing teams real-time confidence at every step of a claim's lifecycle — from entry to adjudication — without requiring retraining?" Prioritised features on an impact-effort matrix in collaboration with product and engineering leads.

3 User Personas Journey Mapping HMW Statements Impact–Effort Matrix Information Architecture
03 · Design

Redesigning the Claim Lifecycle

Consolidated 3 legacy screens into a single unified claim workspace with a contextual sidebar. Designed an inline ICD/CPT code validator with real-time error flagging before submission. Built a claim timeline component showing adjudication status at a glance. Used Claude.design and Figma Make to rapidly generate layout alternatives and validate information hierarchy. Delivered a full design system in Figma with 80+ reusable components documented in Storybook for seamless developer handoff.

Unified Workspace Real-time Validation Design System · 80+ Components Storybook Docs WCAG 2.1 AA
04 · Prototype

Interactive Prototyping & Stakeholder Validation

Built a high-fidelity clickthrough prototype in Figma covering 5 core flows: new claim entry, claim edit & resubmission, batch processing, ERA reconciliation, and dashboard reporting. Used Lovable to create a live coded prototype for executive demos — enabling non-technical stakeholders to interact with the real UI in a browser. Ran 3 rounds of internal review with product, engineering, and compliance, iterating on the status workflow and notification system each cycle.

5 Core Flows Figma Prototype Lovable Live Demo 3 Iteration Rounds
05 · Testing

Usability Testing & Outcomes

Conducted moderated usability testing with 8 billing specialists across 2 sessions. Measured task completion rate, time-on-task, and error rate for the claim submission flow. Post-launch metrics showed a dramatic improvement — claim submission time dropped from an average of 4.2 minutes to 1.8 minutes, the rejection rate fell by 22 percentage points in the first quarter, and user satisfaction score (CSAT) rose from 54 to 81.

E-commerce · Life Sciences

E-commerce Platform for Life Science Products

Designing a dual-tier B2B purchasing experience for high-value laboratory instruments and low-value consumables — serving researchers, procurement managers, and lab technicians.

My RoleLead UX / Product Designer
Duration8 Months
PlatformWeb (Responsive) · B2B E-commerce
ToolsFigma · Claude.ai · Figma Make · Miro
01 · Research

Two Buyer Modes, One Platform

Conducted contextual inquiry sessions with 15 users spanning research scientists, lab procurement officers, and department heads at life science organisations. Leveraged Claude.ai to synthesise purchase behaviour patterns and segment insights by product tier. Key finding: high-value purchases (microscopes, sequencers, centrifuges — $5K–$500K) involve a 3-to-6 person approval chain and 2–8 week decision cycles, while low-value consumables (reagents, pipette tips, gloves) are replenished weekly by a single technician. These two modes required fundamentally different UX journeys within the same platform.

2Distinct buyer modes
6 wksHigh-value decision cycle
15User interviews
3–6Stakeholders per high-value purchase
02 · Define

Separating the Two Purchase Journeys

Defined four personas: the Researcher (explores, compares specs), the Lab Tech (quick reorder, volume buying), the Procurement Officer (approval workflows, budget tracking), and the Department Head (analytics, vendor management). Mapped two separate purchase journeys — a Considered Purchase Flow for high-value items with quote requests, comparison tools, and multi-approver sign-off, and a Quick Reorder Flow for consumables with saved lists, bulk add-to-cart, and subscription ordering. Documented 9 design principles guiding the dual-mode architecture.

4 Personas Dual Journey Architecture Quote Request Flow Quick Reorder Flow 9 Design Principles
03 · Design

A Platform That Knows What You're Buying

Designed an adaptive product detail page that intelligently switches between a High-Value Mode (deep spec tables, document downloads, comparison drawer, quote CTA, demo scheduling) and a Consumable Mode (quantity selector, pack-size variants, reorder history, subscribe-and-save). Built a multi-approver checkout with role-based permission states — requesters, approvers, and budget holders each see a tailored view. Used Figma Make to generate and evaluate 12 layout variants for the product catalogue grid. Delivered 120+ Figma components covering both product tiers, account management, and the approval dashboard.

Adaptive PDP Multi-approver Checkout Subscribe & Save 120+ Components Role-based UI
04 · Prototype

Simulating Both Purchase Journeys

Built two parallel Figma prototypes — one for the High-Value considered purchase journey (browse → compare → request quote → multi-approver review → PO generation) and one for the Quick Reorder journey (saved list → bulk add → express checkout → subscription confirm). Ran scenario-based walkthroughs with a procurement manager and a lab technician simultaneously to expose conflicts in shared components (navigation, cart, account) that needed to serve both modes without confusion.

2 Parallel Prototypes Scenario Walkthroughs Quote → PO Flow Express Checkout
05 · Testing

Validating Across Both Buyer Types

Ran two separate rounds of moderated usability testing — one with 6 procurement/researcher profiles for the high-value flow, one with 5 lab technicians for the reorder flow. Key improvements from testing: the comparison drawer needed a sticky anchor, the approval notification email needed an in-context preview, and the quick-reorder list needed stronger visual differentiation from the standard cart. Post-launch: high-value quote conversion increased by 31%, repeat consumable orders grew by 44%, and average procurement approval time dropped from 9 days to 3.5 days.

AI Product · E-commerce · Life Sciences

AI-Based Agent for E-commerce Content Generation

Designing the UX for an AI-powered content generation agent that automatically produces product titles, descriptions, spec tables, and SEO metadata for 50,000+ life science SKUs — cutting content production time by 80%.

My RoleLead Product Designer · AI Workflow Designer
Duration5 Months
PlatformInternal Web Tool · AI SaaS
ToolsClaude.ai · Figma · Figma Make · Claude.design · Lovable
01 · Research

The Content Bottleneck Problem

The life science e-commerce platform had a critical content gap — over 50,000 product SKUs with incomplete, inconsistent, or missing product descriptions, causing low discoverability and high bounce rates on product pages. Through stakeholder interviews with content managers, catalogue editors, and product owners, and supported by Claude.ai-powered transcript analysis, we identified that a single content editor could produce ~20 quality product descriptions per day — meaning full catalogue coverage would take 7 years. The research also revealed three key constraints: scientific accuracy was non-negotiable, brand voice consistency was critical, and editors needed to trust and control AI-generated content rather than simply accept it.

50K+SKUs needing content
7 yrsManual completion time
20Descriptions/editor/day
3Critical trust constraints
02 · Define

Designing for Human-AI Collaboration

Defined three primary personas: the Content Editor (reviews and approves AI output), the Catalogue Manager (monitors batch progress and quality scores), and the Product Owner (sets brand voice, tone rules, and output templates). The core design challenge was framing: "How might we make AI-generated content feel like a trusted first draft rather than an unreliable black box?" We defined a principle-led framework — Transparency (show confidence scores), Control (editable inline), and Auditability (track every human edit vs AI output). Mapped 4 workflow modes: single-item generation, batch generation, bulk review & edit, and publish workflow with approval gates.

3 Personas Human-AI Collab Model Confidence Scoring 4 Workflow Modes Approval Gates
03 · Design

The AI Content Workspace

Designed a split-panel AI workspace: left panel shows raw product data (SKU, attributes, existing specs), right panel shows the live AI-generated content with field-level confidence indicators (green/amber/red). Each generated field is independently editable with change tracking. Built a Tone & Template Studio where brand managers configure voice guidelines and output structure — feeding directly into the AI prompt pipeline. Designed a Batch Review Grid for processing 100–500 SKUs simultaneously with a accept/edit/reject triage system. Used Claude.design for rapid UI exploration of the confidence scoring visualisations and Figma Make for component variant generation across the 14 content field types.

Split-panel Workspace Confidence Indicators Tone Studio Batch Review Grid Change Tracking
04 · Prototype

Making AI Feel Tangible

Built a high-fidelity Figma prototype simulating the AI generation experience — including animated typing states, confidence score population, and inline edit interactions. Used Lovable to build a functional coded prototype that integrated with a real Claude.ai API endpoint, allowing content editors to interact with actual AI-generated product descriptions during testing sessions — not just static mockups. This was critical for measuring genuine trust and usability rather than reactions to a simulation. Ran the prototype through 3 sprint review cycles as Scrum Master, incorporating engineering feasibility feedback into each design iteration.

Animated AI States Live Claude.ai Integration Functional Lovable Prototype 3 Sprint Cycles
05 · Testing

Building Trust in AI-Generated Content

Ran 2 rounds of usability testing with 10 content editors using the live Lovable prototype. Primary metric was Trust Score — measured by how often editors accepted AI output without editing (target: >60% acceptance). Initial round revealed that editors distrusted amber-scored fields even when content was accurate — leading to a redesign of the confidence explanation tooltip with specific reasons for the score. Final round showed 71% average acceptance rate and a measured content production throughput of 320 SKUs per editor per day — a 16× improvement. Post-launch, the platform cleared 12,000 SKUs in the first month.
